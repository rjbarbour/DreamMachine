## Q3. Weakly Connected Neural Networks (Hoppensteadt & Izhikevich, 1997): canonical models, phase models, main results

### Takeaway
The book builds a theory of **weakly connected neural networks (WCNNs)**. In these networks synaptic coupling is small compared with intrinsic dynamics, with typical PSPs under 1 mV against a roughly 20 mV gap to threshold. Its main tool is the **canonical model**: if a whole family of Hodgkin–Huxley-type networks can be mapped onto one simple system by a continuous, possibly noninvertible change of variables, results proved for that system hold for every member of the family. The type of bifurcation near which each neuron sits determines the canonical model:
- multiple cusp gives a gradient (Hopfield-like) model;
- multiple Andronov–Hopf gives a complex-valued oscillator model;
- saddle-node on a limit cycle gives the Ermentrout–Kopell theta (Class 1) model;
- weakly connected limit-cycle oscillators give phase models (via Malkin's theorem).

Key results:
- Oscillators interact only when their frequencies are resonant.
- Synaptic anatomy does not guarantee functional coupling.
- An oscillatory analog of the Cohen–Grossberg theorem gives oscillatory associative memory.

### Cited Findings
- **Book facts and stated aim:** ISBN 0-387-94948-8, 1997, ~400 pages, 173 illustrations. The book is "devoted to local and global analysis of weakly connected systems with applications to neurosciences. Using bifurcation theory and canonical models as the major tools of analysis…". Topics include "bifurcations in neuron and brain dynamics, oscillatory neural networks, synaptic organizations of the brain, and the nature of neural codes". — [wcnn.htm](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/wcnn.htm)
- **Table of contents:**
  - Part I (Introduction): 1 Introduction; 2 Bifurcations in Neuron Dynamics; 3 Neural Networks; 4 Introduction to Canonical Models.
  - Part II (Derivation of Canonical Models): 5 Local Analysis of WCNNs; 6 Local Analysis of Singularly Perturbed WCNNs; 7 Local Analysis of Weakly Connected Maps; 8 Saddle-Node on a Limit Cycle; 9 Weakly Connected Oscillators.
  - Part III (Analysis of Canonical Models): 10 Multiple Andronov–Hopf Bifurcation; 11 Multiple Cusp Bifurcation; 12 Quasi-Static Bifurcations; 13 Synaptic Organizations of the Brain.
  — [wcnn.htm](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/wcnn.htm)
- **Canonical model definition:** "a model is canonical for a family if there is a continuous change of variables that transforms any other model from the family into this one… The change of variables does not have to [be] invertible, so the canonical model is usually lower-dimensional, simple, and tractable. Yet, it retains many important features of the family", e.g., multiple attractors carry over. — [Canonical Neural Models (Arbib Handbook, 2002), arbib.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/arbib.pdf)
- **Canonical models listed in the Arbib review:**
  - *Multiple cusp / bistability:* y_i' = r_i − y_i³ + Σ_j s_ij y_j. It has the energy function E = −Σ(r_i y_i − y_i⁴/4) − ½ Σ s_ij y_i y_j, so with symmetric connections it is a gradient system like Hopfield's.
  - *Multiple supercritical Andronov–Hopf:* z_i' = (r_i + iω_i) z_i − z_i|z_i|² + Σ_j c_ij z_j, with z_i ∈ ℂ and complex synaptic coefficients c_ij.
  - *Large-amplitude oscillators:* the phase model φ_i' = ω_i + Σ_j H_ij(φ_j − φ_i), with H_ij computed explicitly via Malkin's theorem (Theorem 9.2 of the book).
  — [arbib.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/arbib.pdf)
- **Synchronization / oscillatory associative memory theorems:**
  - For the Hopf model with equal frequencies and a self-adjoint C (c_ij = c̄_ji), the network always converges to a phase-locked pattern. The proof uses an orbital energy function. The book also proves an analog of the Cohen–Grossberg convergence theorem.
  - For the phase model with equal ω and pairwise-odd H_ij (H_ij(−ψ) = −H_ji(ψ)), the network converges to phase-locked patterns via the energy E(φ) = ½ Σ R_ij(φ_j − φ_i) (Theorem 9.15 of the book). Kuramoto's model with s_ij = s_ji and ψ_ij = −ψ_ji is a special case.
  - Memories are stored with a **complex Hebbian rule** c_ij = (k/n) Σ_s ξ_i^s ξ̄_j^s. Memorized images are **limit-cycle (phase-locked) attractors rather than equilibria**, and the "mirror image" problem of Hopfield nets disappears.
  — [arbib.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/arbib.pdf); [pllnn.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/pllnn.pdf)
- **Bio. Cybern. 1996 I (Andronov–Hopf canonical model).** Assumptions: excitatory–inhibitory oscillator pairs, each near a supercritical Andronov–Hopf point, weakly coupled. Results:
  - (1) "A neural oscillator can communicate only with those oscillators which have roughly the same natural frequency".
  - (2) Some "pathological synaptic configurations" block communication even at equal frequency, so "the anatomical presence of synaptic connections… does not necessarily guarantee that the connections are functionally significant".
  - (3) Large phase lags can arise from synaptic organization rather than transmission delays.
  - (4) The model shows self-ignition and oscillator death.
  — [bc1.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/bc1.pdf)
- **Bio. Cybern. 1996 II (learning phase information).** Assumptions: local, slow learning with forgetting. Results: networks that memorize phase information need (1) plasticity rates set by the presynaptic neuron, and (2) excitatory long-axon relay neurons. (3) If inhibitory neurons also have long axons, the network can "learn, passively forget and actively unlearn". — [bc2.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/bc2.pdf)
- **Weak-connectivity justification:** Average PSPs are under 1 mV, compared with about 20 mV to discharge and about 100 mV spike amplitude. Hippocampal PSPs are about 0.1 mV; rat visual cortex PSPs range 0.05–2.08 mV. — [Multiple Cusp Bifurcations, cusp.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/cusp.pdf)
- **Multiple Cusp Bifurcations (1998):** WCNNs near cusp points that satisfy an adaptation condition can be transformed into a canonical model. Depending on parameters, that model works "as a multiple attractor neural network or as a globally asymptotically stable neural network". Keywords include Hebbian learning and "bistability of perception". — [cusp.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/cusp.pdf)
- **Phase models with explicit time delays (PRE 1998):** A delay comparable to one oscillation period has no effect in the weakly coupled phase model and can be neglected. Explicit delays appear in the phase model only when the delay is comparable to 1/ε periods, where ε ≪ 1 is the coupling strength. — [phasedel.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/phasedel.pdf)
- **Burster classification in the book:** The 2000 IJBC review says "a complete classification of all 16 planar point–cycle bursters, though without the naming scheme, was first provided by Hoppensteadt and Izhikevich [1997, Sec. 2.9]". — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)

## Q4. Oscillatory neurocomputers, synchronization-based computation, PLL networks and FM interactions

### Takeaway
From 1998 to 2001 Hoppensteadt and Izhikevich turned the WCNN theory into an engineering proposal. In an **oscillatory neurocomputer**, memories are phase-locked patterns. Interaction happens only between frequency-resonant units ("FM interactions": frequency is the channel and phase deviations carry the information). An external input to a shared medium can impose **dynamic connectivity**, so the n² programmable connections of a conventional design are not needed. They argued that any oscillator can serve as the hardware, including PLL/VCO circuits, lasers, MEMS, Josephson junctions and macromolecules.

### Cited Findings
- **FM-radio hypothesis (BioSystems 1998).** Assumptions: cortical columns are autonomous oscillators weakly connected to the thalamus. Results:
  - Columns "interact only when there is a certain nearly resonant relation between their frequencies… the interactions are frequency modulated (FM)… The frequency of oscillation encodes the channel of communication, while the information is transmitted via phase modulations."
  - A thalamic input of suitable frequency "can dynamically link any two cortical columns, even those that have non-resonant frequencies".
  - The mean firing rate "does not carry any information other than identifying a channel"; the neural code is in modulations of interspike intervals.
  — [fmradio.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/fmradio.pdf)
- **Oscillatory neurocomputers with dynamic connectivity (PRL 1999):** The architecture consists of "oscillators having different frequencies… connected weakly via a common medium forced by an external input. Even though such oscillators are all interconnected homogeneously, the external input imposes a dynamic connectivity." The paper uses Kuramoto's model to prove oscillatory associative properties and notes that the design avoids n² programmable connections. Proposed substrates: VCOs, optical oscillators, lasers, MEMS, Josephson junctions, macromolecules. Provisional patent 60/108,353. — [oscomp.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/oscomp.pdf)
- **Quasi-periodic oscillators (SIAM J Appl Math 1999):** WCNNs of multifrequency (quasi-periodic) oscillators reduce to a phase model with vector phases. With mutually nonresonant frequency vectors "the phase model uncouples… such oscillators do not interact even though there might be physical connections". With low-order resonances they interact through phase deviations, which "resembles that of the FM radio, with a shared feature—multiplexing of signals". — [quasi.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/quasi.pdf)
- **Weakly pulse-coupled oscillators (IEEE TNN 1999, tutorial).** Assumptions: neurons fire periodically and are weakly connected. Such pulse-coupled networks reduce, by a piecewise-continuous change of variables, to a phase model, which can predict whether the network has oscillatory associative memory. The paper derives simple pulse-coupled networks that "memorize and reproduce synchronized temporal patterns the same way a Hopfield network does with static patterns", learning through synaptic weights and/or transmission delays. — [pco.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/pco.pdf)
- **PLL neural networks (IEEE TNN 2000):** The architecture is built from phase-locked-loop circuits. It "stores and retrieves complex oscillatory patterns as synchronized states with appropriate phase relations". It relies on the oscillatory Cohen–Grossberg analog (book Thm 9.15): oscillatory networks "can have all the neurocomputational properties of standard Hopfield networks, with a key exception: Memorized patterns are not equilibria, but synchronized oscillatory states". Interaction is by phase modulation (PM) and depends on resonant frequency relations. — [pllnn.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/pllnn.pdf)
- **Laser oscillators (PRE 2000):** In this optical neurocomputing scheme, lasers are neurons and a holographic medium holds the connections. Neurons communicate via phases (PM) rather than intensities (AM). "Memorized patterns correspond to synchronized states… The mechanism of recognition is related to phase locking." — [lasers.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/lasers.pdf)
- **MEMS resonators (IEEE TCAS-I 2001):** The paper derives a simple phase-locking model for low-amplitude MEMS oscillators. It shows that MEMS alone could build an oscillatory neurocomputer with autocorrelative associative memory (US provisional patent 60/178,654). — [mems.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/mems.pdf)
- **Phase equations for relaxation oscillators (SIAM JAM 2000):** Using Malkin's theorem, the paper finds explicit connection functions when the slow variable is one-dimensional. These become discontinuous in the relaxation limit μ → 0, which gives a simple alternative illustration of Somers–Kopell fast threshold modulation (FTM). The paper points out that weakly coupled relaxation oscillators still need O(1/ε) cycles to synchronize. — [prel.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/prel.pdf)
- **Slowly coupled oscillators (SIAM JAM 2003, with Hoppensteadt):** The paper extends Frankel–Kiemel to slowly coupled networks and derives a canonical phase model via Malkin's theorem. It covers Andronov–Hopf, SNIC (Class 1) and relaxation examples, and compares slow coupling with weak coupling. — [slow.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/slow.pdf)

### Inferences
- The neurocomputer papers are hardware-agnostic consequences of the same theorem: anything near a Hopf point, or any limit-cycle oscillator with suitably odd coupling, gives a phase-locking associative memory. The papers state this outright ("anything that can oscillate can also be used for computing", [arbib.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/arbib.pdf)).
- This line of work, phase-based associative memory in oscillator hardware, anticipates the later "oscillatory neural network" and "Ising machine" hardware literature. That is my observation; I did not research it here.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence that any of the proposed hardware neurocomputers (PLL, laser or MEMS) was built by the authors.
- The fates of the provisional patents (conversion to full patents) were not found.

## Q5. "Neural Excitability, Spiking and Bursting" (IJBC 2000): Hodgkin classes, integrators vs resonators, burster classification

### Takeaway
This 96-page tutorial review (with 126 figures) argues that **the bifurcation of the rest state determines a neuron's computational properties**:
- Near a **saddle-node (on invariant circle)** bifurcation, the cell is Class 1, fires at arbitrarily low frequency, has a well-defined threshold and is an **integrator**.
- Near an **Andronov–Hopf** bifurcation, the cell is Class 2, fires within a frequency band, has no sharp threshold, can fire on rebound from inhibition and is a **resonator**.

It also introduces the **"rest-bifurcation / spiking-bifurcation"** naming scheme for bursters. Pairing the 12 relevant codimension-1 bifurcations of rest with the 10 of spiking gives **120 types of codimension-1 fast–slow bursters**. Only **24 of these are planar** (16 point–cycle and 8 cycle–cycle), and many are new.

### Cited Findings
- **Abstract:** "when the rest state is near a saddle-node bifurcation, the cell can fire all-or-none spikes with an arbitrary low frequency, it has a well-defined threshold manifold, and it acts as an integrator; i.e. the higher the frequency of incoming pulses, the sooner it fires. In contrast, when the rest state is near an Andronov–Hopf bifurcation, the cell fires in a certain frequency range, its spikes are not all-or-none, it does not have a well-defined threshold manifold, it can fire in response to an inhibitory pulse, and it acts as a resonator; i.e. it responds preferentially to a certain (resonant) frequency of the input. Increasing the input frequency may actually delay or terminate its firing." — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Publication details:** IJBC 10(6):1171–1266. Received June 1999; affiliations NSI and ASU. — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Hodgkin (1948) classes as presented in the paper:**
  - Class 1: "Action potentials can be generated with arbitrarily low frequency"; frequencies range smoothly over about 5–150 Hz.
  - Class 2: "generated in a certain frequency band that is relatively insensitive to changes in the strength of the applied current", typically 75–150 Hz.
  - The qualitative distinction is zero vs. nonzero frequency of the emerging oscillation, reflecting different bifurcations. A saddle-node on invariant circle "always results in Class 1 excitability". The classic Hodgkin–Huxley model "does not exhibit Class 1 excitability unless the potassium A-current is added".
  — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Further neurocomputational contrasts:** "Class 1 excitatory neurons never fire in response to" inhibitory pulses (with caveats). "Identical Class 1 excitable neurons are difficult [to synchronize]." Excitatory and inhibitory pulses move a Class 1 neuron toward or away from threshold, while for Class 2 neurons both can decrease the distance to threshold depending on timing. — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Ermentrout–Kopell theorem:** The paper presents the theorem as the canonical model for Class 1 excitability (the theta/"Ermentrout–Kopell canonical model"), citing Hoppensteadt & Izhikevich 1997. — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Burster count:** "Since there are 12 relevant codimension 1 bifurcations of the quiescent state and 10 relevant codimension 1 bifurcations of the spiking state, there are 120 different types of codimension 1 bursters; see Table 4… there are only 24 various types of planar codimension 1 fast–slow bursters" (Table 3). Bursters are "point–cycle" when the quiescent state is an equilibrium and "cycle–cycle" when it is a subthreshold oscillation. — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Mapping to classic burster names:**

  | Izhikevich name | Older names |
  |---|---|
  | fold/homoclinic | square-wave, Type I |
  | circle/circle | parabolic, Type II |
  | subHopf/fold cycle | elliptic, Type III |
  | fold/fold cycle | Type IV |
  | fold/Hopf | tapered, Type V |
  | fold/circle | triangular |

  "Many other bursting types listed in Table 3 are new." The history runs from Rinzel 1987 (square-wave, parabolic, elliptic) to Bertram et al. 1995 to Rush & Rinzel 1994 (triangular): "the total number of identified bursters to be 6". — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Top-down method:** "They use a bottom–up approach… In contrast, we use the top–down approach: We consider all possible pairs of codimension 1 bifurcations of rest and spiking states… and then we invent a conductance-based model exhibiting each bursting type. Thus, many of our bursters are 'theoretical' in the sense that they have yet to be seen in experiments." — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Interacting bursters:** The paper also shows "that different bursters can interact, synchronize and process information differently". — [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf)
- **Subcritical elliptic bursting of Bautin type (SIAM JAM 2000):** The paper proves a canonical model for elliptic (subHopf / double-limit-cycle) bursters near a Bautin (generalized Hopf) point, and for weakly connected networks of them. Such networks behave "quite different[ly] from… weakly connected phase oscillators, and… resemble… strongly connected relaxation oscillators". They "need few (usually one) bursts to synchronize". In-phase synchronization is robust to heterogeneity, while out-of-phase synchronization is hard. Interactions are "most effective when the presynaptic interspike frequency matches the frequency of postsynaptic oscillations". — [subEB.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/subEB.pdf)
- **SIAM Review 2001 (SIGEST):** This is a condensed reprint of the Bautin paper, with proofs omitted and FitzHugh–Rinzel, Morris–Lecar and Hodgkin–Huxley simulations added. PUBS describes it as a "Featured SIGEST paper selected on the basis of its exceptional interest to the entire SIAM community". — [sirev.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/sirev.pdf); [PUBS](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/)
- **Classification of Bursting Mappings (IJBC 2004, with Hoppensteadt):** extends the classification to discrete-time maps. Listed on PUBS; not read in detail. — [PUBS](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/)

### Inferences
- The integrator/resonator split and the 120-burster taxonomy are the conceptual core of his 2007 MIT Press book (covered by other researchers). The IJBC 2000 paper is the direct precursor.
- This review is his most-cited pre-2003 paper (see Q7).

### Gaps
- The full list of the 12 rest and 10 spiking codimension-1 bifurcations (Table 4) was not transcribed, because the table did not survive PDF text extraction. Readers can consult Tables 1, 2 and 4 of [nesb.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/nesb.pdf).
- The paper says "Hodgkin classes 1 and 2". Whether it also discusses Class 3 was not confirmed in the text extracted.

## Q6. Resonate-and-fire (2001), Class 1 pulse-coupled foundations (1999), bursts as a unit of information (2002–03)

### Takeaway
Three linked contributions:
- **The 1999 Class 1 paper** proves that a broad class of biophysical networks reduces to a canonical pulse-coupled (integrate-and-fire-like) model by a change of coordinates. The required conditions are weak connectivity, Class 1 excitability and conventional synapses. This makes pulse-coupled models mathematically legitimate rather than "toy" models.
- **Resonate-and-fire (2001)** is the resonator counterpart of integrate-and-fire: a linear oscillator with a complex state variable and a firing threshold on Im z.
- **The 2002–03 resonance papers** argue that bursts enable *selective communication*. The interspike frequency within a burst determines which postsynaptic resonators respond, through subthreshold oscillations or short-term synaptic dynamics.

### Cited Findings
- **Class 1 pulse-coupled foundations (IEEE TNN 1999).** Claim: "a huge class of biophysically detailed and biologically plausible neural-network models can be transformed into a canonical pulse-coupled form by a piece-wise continuous, possibly noninvertible, change of variables". Conditions: the network is weakly connected, neurons are Class 1 excitable, and synapses are conventional (axo-dendritic and axo-somatic). Consequence: "the difference between studying the pulse-coupled model and Hodgkin–Huxley-type neural networks is just a matter of a coordinate change". It shows that the pulse-coupled network of identical neurons does not synchronize in-phase, which "confirms Ermentrout's result that weakly connected Class 1 neurons are difficult to synchronize, regardless of the equations". Keywords: saddle-node on limit cycle, desynchronization, integrate-and-fire. — [pulse.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/pulse.pdf)
- **Resonate-and-fire neurons (Neural Networks 2001).** The model is "similar to the integrate-and-fire neuron except that the state variable is complex". It captures resonator phenomena:
  - preference for input near the eigenfrequency;
  - excitation or inhibition by a doublet depending on its interspike interval;
  - firing in response to inhibitory input.
  It is "computationally efficient and suitable for simulations of large networks of spiking neurons". The neuron fires when y = Im z crosses the threshold y = 1. Keywords: Andronov–Hopf bifurcation, FM interactions. — [resfire.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/resfire.pdf)
- **Resonance and selective communication via bursts (BioSystems 2002).** The paper contrasts its view with Lisman's (1997) reliability hypothesis. In a postsynaptic cell with damped oscillations, responses are amplified "when the intra-burst frequency equals the cell's eigenfrequency. Responses are negligible, however, if the intra-burst frequency is twice the eigenfrequency." Hence "the same burst could be effective for one cell and ineffective for another", and bursts "may provide effective mechanisms for selective communication". — [bios02.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/bios02.pdf)
- **Bursts as a unit of neural information (TINS 2003, with Desai, Walcott, Hoppensteadt).** The review frames itself as complementary to Lisman (1997). The classical view holds that shorter interspike intervals within a burst are always better; the authors call that "only half of the story". Frequency preference exists at two levels:
  - synaptic, from competing short-term depression and facilitation;
  - neuronal, from subthreshold membrane-potential oscillations or resonant currents.
  A presynaptic cell can therefore "selectively affect some postsynaptic targets, but not others" by changing the intraburst frequency "without changing the efficacy of synapses". The illustrations use rat layer-5 somatosensory pyramidal and bipolar-interneuron recordings. — [tins.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/tins.pdf)
- **Synchronization of elliptic bursters (2000/2001):** The same resonance principle appears at the network level: burster interactions are most effective when presynaptic interspike frequency matches postsynaptic oscillation frequency. — [sirev.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/sirev.pdf)

### Inferences
- The integrator/resonator dichotomy runs through all of these papers:
  - Class 1 neurons (SNIC) get the integrate-and-fire canonical form (1999).
  - Class 2 neurons (Hopf) get the resonate-and-fire form (2001).
  - Resonators enable frequency-selective, multiplexed communication: bursts in 2002–03, FM interactions in 1998–99.
- The 2003 "simple model" (covered by others) combines integrator and resonator behaviour in one 2-D system. That makes the resonate-and-fire paper a direct conceptual predecessor.

### Gaps
- The exact resonate-and-fire equation (z' = (b + iω)z + I with reset) was confirmed only partially from the text extraction (complex state, threshold on Im z = 1). The precise parameterization and reset rule should be checked in [resfire.pdf](https://www.izhikevich.org/publications/resfire.pdf).
