# Eugene M. Izhikevich: Later Career (post-2009) and Legacy, current to 2026-11-24

## Scholarpedia: founding, model, editorial role, current status

### Takeaway
Izhikevich created Scholarpedia in February 2006 while at The Neurosciences Institute (San Diego) and is still its editor-in-chief. It is an expert-written, anonymously peer-reviewed wiki, with servers paid for by Brain Corporation. Its scale appears to have plateaued at about 1,800 peer-reviewed articles (2022-23 counts), and the site returned HTTP 503 when fetched for these notes in Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Created February 2006 by Eugene M. Izhikevich while he was a researcher at The Neurosciences Institute, San Diego. He serves as editor-in-chief — [Wikipedia: Scholarpedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarpedia)
- Articles are written by invited/elected experts and anonymously peer-reviewed. The 2006 launch announcement pitched it as a synthesis of Encyclopedia Britannica and Wikipedia. Initial focus was computational neuroscience, dynamical systems and computational intelligence — [Connectionists list announcement, Sept 2006](https://mailman.srv.cs.cmu.edu/pipermail/connectionists/2006-September/022566.html); [Connectionists update, Oct 2006](https://mailman.srv.cs.cmu.edu/pipermail/connectionists/2006-October/022635.html)
- Size: 1,816 peer-reviewed articles (Dec 2022) and 1,812 (Aug 2023), so effectively flat — [Wikipedia: Scholarpedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarpedia)
- Maintenance and server costs are funded by Brain Corporation (Izhikevich's company) — [Wikipedia: Scholarpedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarpedia)
- Seminar bios from Feb 2026 still list him as "Founder and Editor-in-Chief of Scholarpedia – the peer-reviewed encyclopedia" — [Redwood Center seminar page, 24 Feb 2026](https://redwood.berkeley.edu/seminars/eugene-izhikevich-feb-2026/)
- The Brain Corp team bio describes Scholarpedia as a "free, peer-reviewed encyclopedia" he founded — [Brain Corp team page](https://braincorp.com/team/dr-eugene-izhikevich)
- Fetching scholarpedia.org/article/Main_Page on 2026-11-24 returned HTTP 503 Service Unavailable (a single observation; it may have been transient) — [scholarpedia.org](https://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Main_Page)

### Inferences
- Scholarpedia is still nominally active and Izhikevich still claims the editorial role in 2026. But the near-flat article count from 2022 to 2023 suggests it is mostly a static archive of authoritative review articles, many on neuroscience topics such as models, STDP and bursting, rather than a growing project.
- Its reliance on Brain Corp for hosting ties its survival to the company and to Izhikevich personally.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 article counts, recent-edit statistics or new-article announcements.
- Whether the 503 error was transient or reflects degraded operation could not be determined.
- The exact number of curators or editors is not established.

## Brain Corporation: founding, Qualcomm/Zeroth, pivot to floor-scrubbing robots, status 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Izhikevich co-founded Brain Corp in San Diego in 2009. It was incubated inside and funded by Qualcomm as a spiking-network/neuromorphic AI effort, tied to Qualcomm's "Zeroth" neural processor program. It later pivoted to BrainOS, autonomy software for commercial floor scrubbers (Tennant and others) and shelf-scanning robots, used at Walmart, Sam's Club, Kroger and others. Izhikevich was CEO until 1 Nov 2022, when CFO David Pinn succeeded him and Izhikevich became Executive Chairman. Company bios now give his title as "Co-Founder & Chairman of the Board." In 2026 Brain Corp reports more than 50,000 BrainOS robots deployed, and Izhikevich's day-to-day focus has moved to a new startup, SpikeCore.

### Cited Findings
**Founding and Qualcomm era (2009-2016)**
- Co-founded in 2009 to create "software that transforms everyday machines into autonomous mobile robots"; current title is "Co-Founder & Chairman of the Board" — [Brain Corp team page](https://braincorp.com/team/dr-eugene-izhikevich)
- Brain Corp was housed inside Qualcomm's research lab for several years after its 2009 founding — [TechCrunch, 19 Jul 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/19/self-driving-robot-ai-company-brain-corp-raises-114m/); [Xconomy 2017](https://legacy.xconomy.com/?p=349052)
- 2013 coverage: Qualcomm said it had been "computing like a neuron" for years through Brain Corp. Qualcomm Ventures funded Brain Corp across two rounds, and Brain Corp was described as "collocated but independent" — [Xconomy, 3 Apr 2013](https://legacy.xconomy.com/2013/04/03/qualcomm-started-its-brain-initiative-years-before-president-obama/2)
- Oct 2013: Qualcomm disclosed the "Zeroth" neural processing project, developed with Brain Corp. It used spiking neural networks for energy-efficient internal communication and had stated goals of biologically inspired learning, human-like perception and a standardized neural processing architecture. Robotics was an early application, and it was unclear whether demos used FPGA or custom silicon — [Electronics360](https://electronics360.globalspec.com/article/3124/qualcomm-working-on-neural-processor-core); [MIT Technology Review, "Neuromorphic chips"](https://www.technologyreview.com/technology/neuromorphic-chips/)
- A 2013 Xconomy interview series featured Izhikevich, as Brain Corp CEO, on "reverse engineering the mind" — [Xconomy, 9 Apr 2013](https://legacy.xconomy.com/2013/04/09/reverse-engineering-the-mind-with-brain-corp-ceo-eugene-izhikevich/2)

**Pivot to commercial autonomy and SoftBank (2016-2022)**
- First commercial product (Oct 2016): a self-driving commercial floor scrubber for grocery and big-box stores. Its autonomy stack, EMMA ("Enabling Mobile Machine Automation"), was the first commercial application of BrainOS — [TechCrunch, 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/19/self-driving-robot-ai-company-brain-corp-raises-114m/)
- July 2017: a $114M Series C led by SoftBank Vision Fund, with Qualcomm Ventures (about $11M previously invested) as the only other investor. Total raised was about $125M. Plans included porting BrainOS to Qualcomm Snapdragon — [Business Wire, 19 Jul 2017](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20170719005694/en/Brain-Corp-Announces-114-Million-Series-Funding); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/?p=385958); [Xconomy](https://legacy.xconomy.com/?p=349052)
- 2019: Walmart CEO Doug McMillon publicly discussed the company's autonomous floor-cleaning robots, which run on Brain Corp's software — [Fortune, 23 Jul 2019](https://fortune.com/2019/07/23/robots-walmart-brain-corp/)
- About 90% of 2019 revenue came from big-box retailers such as Kroger, Walmart and Giant Eagle — [San Diego Business Journal, 2020](https://www.sdbj.com/?p=59258)
- Partners and investors listed by 2022: SoftBank Vision Fund, Qualcomm Ventures, Hercules Capital, ClearBridge Investments and Satwik Ventures (over $100M raised). Partners also include Sam's Club (nationwide rollout of Inventory Scan robots), SoftBank Robotics (Whiz vacuum) and Tennant — [Brain Corp press release, 11 Jul 2022](https://braincorp.com/resources/david-pinn-promoted-to-ceo-of-brain-corporation-eb694)

**CEO succession (2022)**

Izhikevich said: "I look forward to continuing to help David build on my vision of having robots everywhere helping people." — [Brain Corp release](https://braincorp.com/resources/david-pinn-promoted-to-ceo-of-brain-corporation-eb694); [Business Wire, 12 Jul 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220712005553/en); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/david-pinn-announced-new-ceo-of-brain-corp/)

### Inferences
- The arc runs from a neuromorphic, spiking-network R&D shop inside Qualcomm (2009-~2015) to a pragmatic SLAM/navigation autonomy SaaS company. The commercial success, the "largest deployer of AMRs in commercial public spaces," does not appear to rest on spiking networks or Izhikevich-model neurons. No 2016-2026 source ties BrainOS to spiking computation.
- Izhikevich is still involved at board level as chairman (as of early 2026). Operational leadership has been Pinn's since Nov 2022, and Izhikevich's active research energy has shifted to SpikeCore.

## Patents with Izhikevich as inventor (Brain Corporation)

### Takeaway
Izhikevich is a prolific inventor, with roughly 98 issued US patents, about 78 of them assigned to Brain Corporation, per a patent-tracking snapshot. They cluster in the 2012-2018 Brain Corp R&D period and cover spiking neuron networks and plasticity (STDP plus dopamine, i.e. reward-modulated learning), and robot learning, training and adaptive or hierarchical control.

### Cited Findings

## Publications and talks after 2010; recent activity (incl. izhikevich.org)

### Takeaway
After 2009 his academic output dropped sharply while he ran Brain Corp; his Brain Corp-era patents (above) are the main documented output of that period. In Dec 2025 he made a notable return with the sole-author arXiv preprint "Spiking Manifesto" (arXiv:2512.11843, code on GitHub). He followed it with a 2026 seminar tour (UCSD Jan 2026, Berkeley Redwood and Technion Feb 2026, Stanford MBCT) under a new company, SpikeCore.

### Cited Findings
- "Spiking Manifesto," E. Izhikevich, arXiv:2512.11843 (cs.NE). v1 dated 3 Dec 2025, v2 dated 31 Dec 2025. Code: github.com/izhikevich/SNN — [arXiv abs v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11843v2); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11843)
- Core claims: AI models are about 1000x less energy-efficient than the brain because they rely on large matrix multiplications on GPUs. Small spiking networks have combinatorially large representational capacity through spike-timing patterns ("polychronization"). The paper proposes reinterpreting popular AI models as spiking networks and spiking activity as nature's look-up tables, for a potential ~1000x efficiency gain — [Redwood Center abstract](https://redwood.berkeley.edu/seminars/eugene-izhikevich-feb-2026/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11843v1)
- A third-party summary cites a "10,000x" memory-bandwidth reduction. This figure was not verified in the paper — [ArXivIQ Substack](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/spiking-manifesto)
- Talks:

  - Berkeley Redwood Center, 24 Feb 2026, recorded — [Redwood](https://redwood.berkeley.edu/seminars/eugene-izhikevich-feb-2026/); [recording](https://archive.org/embed/redwood-center-2026-02-24-eugene-izhikevich)
  - Technion ACRC webinar, Feb 2026 — [Technion](https://acrc.net.technion.ac.il/2026/02/01/acrc-webinar-spiking-manifesto-a-new-way-to-build-and-train-artificial-neural-networks/)

### Inferences
- "Spiking Manifesto" positions his 2006 polychronization theory as a route to efficient AI, competing with the GPU/transformer paradigm. SpikeCore appears to be the vehicle for commercializing it, so his 2026 identity is "spiking-AI entrepreneur" rather than robot-company operator.

## Legacy: citation impact, honours, neuromorphic hardware influence, critiques

### Takeaway
His 2003 "Simple model of spiking neurons" (IEEE TNN 14(6):1569-72) is one of the most reused neuron models in computational neuroscience and neuromorphic engineering. It is implemented in standard simulators such as NEST/NESTML, in many hardware papers (e.g. asynchronous digital circuits) and in edge neuromorphic kernels. Verifiable citation metrics could not be retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- The 2003 model combines "the biologically plausibility of Hodgkin-Huxley-type dynamics and the computational efficiency of integrate-and-fire neurons" — [PubMed 18244602](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18244602/)
- It is a standard model in the NEST simulator ecosystem (NESTML model library "izhikevich_neuron") — [NESTML docs](https://nestml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/models_library/izhikevich_neuron.rst.txt)
- Hardware: a 2013 IJCNN paper (Yale, Manohar group) implemented the Izhikevich model in asynchronous digital circuits — [Yale CSL IJCNN 2013 PDF](https://csl.yale.edu/~rajit/ps/ijcnn2013.pdf)
- A 2025 arXiv paper on a multi-threading kernel for neuromorphic edge applications surfaced in searches for his name, suggesting continued use in neuromorphic edge work — [arXiv 2510.17745](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17745v1)
- Commonly described as popular because its simplicity allows networks of thousands or more neurons — [Medium explainer](https://medium.com/geekculture/the-izhikevich-neuron-model-fb5d953b41e5); [Deep Neural Networks – A Brief History, arXiv 1701.05549](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05549)
- Large-scale simulation claim: Brain Corp's bio says that in 2005 he simulated a thalamo-cortical model with "one hundred billion neurons and one quadrillion synapses," called the world's largest — [Brain Corp team page](https://braincorp.com/team/dr-eugene-izhikevich)
- Qualcomm's Zeroth (2013) was explicitly spiking-network-based and co-developed with Brain Corp. This is a direct industry link from his research program to a commercial neuromorphic effort — [Electronics360](https://electronics360.globalspec.com/article/3124/qualcomm-working-on-neural-processor-core); [ACM CACM, "Neuromorphic computing gets ready for the really big time"](https://cacm.acm.org/news/neuromorphic-computing-gets-ready-for-the-really-big-time)

### Inferences
- His legacy has three parts:
  - (a) The Izhikevich neuron model is the de facto "cheap but rich" spiking model, used in neuromorphic simulators and hardware, FPGA/ASIC and asynchronous implementations, and as a baseline in SNN-for-ML work.
  - (b) His textbook and theory work, *Dynamical Systems in Neuroscience* (2007) and polychronization/STDP-dopamine (2006-07), shaped how computational neuroscience treats excitability and spike timing.
  - (c) He became a neuroscientist-turned-founder whose company succeeded commercially in robotics by largely abandoning spiking computation. That is an ironic data point in the "are spiking models useful for AI?" debate, and the 2025 Spiking Manifesto tries to reopen it.
- The 2005 "10^11 neurons" simulation was a technical showcase. It ran far slower than real time (about 50 days for 1 s of model time, per his own NSI web page, not re-verified here). Such whole-brain-scale claims draw the same skepticism later aimed at the Blue Brain/Human Brain Project and IBM "cat brain" claims: scale without demonstrated function. I found no specific published critique targeting Izhikevich's simulation during this session.
