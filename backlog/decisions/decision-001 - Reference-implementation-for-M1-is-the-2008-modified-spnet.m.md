## Context

<!-- SECTION:CONTEXT:BEGIN -->
Izhikevich published MATLAB (spnet.m) and C++ (spnet.cpp) versions of the 2006 polychronization network. His own page says they are similar but not equivalent, and the prior-art note (references/notes/2026-11-24-spnet-replications-and-simulators.md) summarises Pauli et al. 2018 as finding that the two differ (order of the 0.9 decay relative to the weight update; delay assignment) and that neither matches the paper exactly. M1 needs one of them named as the thing we replicate.
<!-- SECTION:CONTEXT:END -->

## Decision

<!-- SECTION:DECISION:BEGIN -->
M1 replicates spnet.m as downloaded on 2026-11-24 (the version modified on 16 April 2008 to allow arbitrary delay distributions; SHA-256 in references/manifest.jsonl under izhikevich_spnet_m). Exact agreement is claimed only against that file, executed under GNU Octave on networks and input sequences generated by our own builder.
<!-- SECTION:DECISION:END -->

## Consequences

<!-- SECTION:CONSEQUENCES:BEGIN -->
Our oracle and kernel reproduce that file's spike trains and weights exactly (docs/validation/crosscheck-original.md). Nothing is claimed about spnet.cpp or about the exact figures in the 2006 paper, which were produced with a different delay assignment and MATLAB's random stream. Statistical comparison with the paper and with the replication study is a separate task (TASK-013).
<!-- SECTION:CONSEQUENCES:END -->

## Alternatives

<!-- SECTION:ALTERNATIVES:BEGIN -->
spnet.cpp: closer to the code used for the published group counts according to the spnet page, but needs a patched build to import our connectivity, and the replication study reports it disagrees with the paper too. The NEST implementation from the replication study: a different simulator with its own numerics, useful as a statistical cross-check but not as an exact reference.
<!-- SECTION:ALTERNATIVES:END -->
